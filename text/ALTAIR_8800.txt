ALTAIR 8800
===========

The Altair 8800 was introduced by MITS in 1975 as a computer kit for
electronics hobbyists. Its appearance on the cover of Popular Electronics
helped bring the emerging microcomputer into public view.

Based on the Intel 8080 processor, the Altair arrived as a kit that buyers
assembled themselves, with an assembled version also available. The basic
front panel used switches for entering data and lights for viewing results.
It did not include a keyboard or screen as a standard user interface, so
owners often added terminals and other peripherals.

The computer's design encouraged expansion. Additional memory and interface
boards could be fitted to the bus, and outside companies soon offered
compatible products. This ecosystem helped turn the Altair into more than a
single kit: it became a platform for experimentation and software.

MITS also offered Altair BASIC, an interpreter that let users write and run
programs. The Altair and its community helped inspire the growth of the
personal-computer industry and many of the clubs and businesses that formed
around microcomputers in the 1970s.
