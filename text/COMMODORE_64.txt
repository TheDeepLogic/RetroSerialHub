COMMODORE 64
============

Commodore introduced the Commodore 64 in 1982. Its name referred to its 64
kilobytes of RAM, an unusually large amount for a relatively affordable home
computer at the time.

The machine combined a MOS Technology 6510 processor with two custom chips
that became central to its character. The VIC-II generated color graphics
and hardware sprites, while the SID chip produced programmable sound. These
capabilities made the C64 popular for games, music, and creative software.

At startup, users encountered Commodore BASIC. The computer could connect to
a television or monitor and supported cassette, cartridge, and disk storage
through its expansion and peripheral ports. Its low price and the scale of
Commodore's manufacturing helped it reach a very large audience.

An active software and hardware community grew around the system. Commercial
games, educational titles, music tools, and user-written programs appeared
throughout its lifetime. The C64 remains especially well known for its
distinctive sound and colorful game library.
