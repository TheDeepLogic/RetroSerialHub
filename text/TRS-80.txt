TRS-80
======

Tandy Corporation introduced the TRS-80 Model I in 1977 through its Radio
Shack stores. The name combined Tandy, Radio Shack, and the Zilog Z80
processor used by the computer. Selling through a familiar retail chain
made the new machine accessible to hobbyists and first-time computer users.

The Model I was sold with a keyboard, a video display, and a version of
BASIC. Early systems used cassette recorders to save and load programs.
Tandy's Expansion Interface added connections for peripherals, including
floppy disk drives and a printer. This let users begin with a basic setup
and add capabilities as needed.

The TRS-80 family grew to include later models with updated processors,
keyboards, storage, and enclosures. The Model III brought many components
together in a more integrated case. Software publishers and independent
developers produced games, business applications, programming tools, and
utilities for the line.

TRS-80 computers were also supported by user groups and magazines. Their
combination of retail availability, approachable BASIC, and expandable
hardware helped make personal computing a hands-on activity for a wide
community.
