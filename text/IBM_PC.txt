IBM PERSONAL COMPUTER
=====================

IBM announced the IBM Personal Computer, model 5150, in 1981. IBM was already
well known for large business systems, so its entry into the personal
computer market brought the new category additional attention from
businesses and software developers.

The 5150 used an Intel 8088 processor and was designed from standard
components. Customers could choose memory, display, and storage options,
and expansion slots allowed additional hardware to be installed. The
computer's operating system was PC DOS, supplied by Microsoft.

IBM published technical information that helped independent companies make
compatible hardware and software. Over time, computers following the
"IBM-compatible" design became a broad industry standard. Many companies
produced systems that could run software written for the IBM PC, expanding
the range of available applications.

The original IBM PC was not the first personal computer, but its market
influence shaped the direction of business computing. Its open, expandable
approach helped establish a platform that continued through many generations
of compatible computers.
